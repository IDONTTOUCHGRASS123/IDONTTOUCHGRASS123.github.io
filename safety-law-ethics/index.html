ETHICS

What should we do?

Ethics asks what is fair and responsible. Respecting consent, collecting only needed data, explaining its use, and reducing harm are design duties—even when a choice is technically possible.

LAW · U.S. FEDERAL

What is legally allowed?

The Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA), 18 U.S.C. § 1030, prohibits specified forms of unauthorized access to protected computers. It is a legal boundary, not a complete ethics checklist. Whether conduct violates the law depends on the facts; a suspicious message alone is not automatically a CFAA violation.

Why pause?

Phishing messages impersonate trusted people or organizations to trick someone into sharing information or taking an unsafe action. Urgency, a mismatched sender or link, and a request for a password or one-time code are reasons to stop and verify independently. Don't reply, open unexpected links, or pass them along.

Ethics and law are related, not identical. Deceiving people and exposing their accounts raises ethical concerns. U.S. federal computer-access law may apply to particular unauthorized access; the facts matter, and this lesson is educational—not legal advice.
